# Wake Morning Playbook (knowledge base for the coach)

Use this to answer quick questions about mornings, energy, focus and planning. Keep answers to one or two spoken sentences, then steer back to today's plan. This is general wellness guidance, not medical advice; for anything medical, tell them to talk to a doctor.

## Getting out of bed
- Grogginess right after waking (sleep inertia) is normal and usually fades within 15 to 30 minutes. Moving, light and water help it pass faster.
- Fastest wake-up moves: feet on the floor, open the blinds or step outside for a few minutes of daylight, drink a glass of water, splash cold water on the face.
- Snoozing splits the last stretch of sleep into short, low-quality chunks, so it usually makes people feel groggier, not more rested. One alarm, then up, beats several snoozes.
- Keeping the same wake time every day, weekends included, is one of the most reliable ways to make mornings easier over a couple of weeks.

## Sleep
- Most college-age adults need about 7 to 9 hours of sleep.
- A consistent bedtime, a dark and cool room, and putting the phone away 30 minutes before bed all help.
- Caffeine late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep; many people cut it off by early afternoon.

## Energy during the day
- Eat something with protein in the morning to stay full longer.
- A short walk or a few minutes of movement between classes resets focus.
- A short nap of 20 minutes or less can boost alertness without much grogginess; long naps late in the day can hurt night sleep.

## Focus and getting work done
- Do the hardest or most important task in the first open block of the day, before email and social media.
- Work in focused blocks: 25 to 50 minutes of work, then a 5 to 10 minute break.
- Put the phone in another room or on Do Not Disturb during a focus block.
- Break a big assignment into the very next small step ("write the intro paragraph") instead of the whole thing.
- If a task has a deadline today, schedule it before anything optional.

## Planning the day
- Leave 10 minutes of buffer between back-to-back things to walk, eat or reset.
- If the day is too full, move or drop the lowest-priority item instead of cutting sleep.
- Exercise is easier to stick with when it has a fixed time on the calendar.

## About Wake
- Wake is an AI wake-up coach built at the SDx SDSU Voice Agent Hackathon. The voice runs on ElevenLabs; each day's plan is computed live by a scheduler running in a Daytona sandbox.
- Users pick one of four coach voices (Hype, Sunny, Calm, Coach), name their coach, connect Google Calendar, and can paste in context from ChatGPT or Claude so the coach knows them.
